Part 1: What are we disagreeing about?

Identify the root issue—not how angry the people look.

Task

Different opinions about the goal, idea, or best decision. Example: Which supplier should we choose?

Process

Disagreement about how work is done, who does what, or when. Example: Who approves the report?

Relationship

Personal tension, trust, respect, or interpersonal friction. Example: Two colleagues repeatedly undermine one another.

Tip: A task conflict can escalate into personal blame. For Part 1, look for the original cause.

What's the Real Cause?

Sometimes conflict starts because of the work environment, not because people are difficult. Five common organizational causes are:

PART 2 · ESCALATION

How far has it escalated?

Now focus on what people are doing, not the original disagreement. The same conflict type can appear at different escalation levels.

0 · CooperationRespectful discussion; working together
1 · TensionFrustration or discomfort appears
2 · Repeated ArgumentsDebates repeat; each tries to win
3 · Personal BlameAttacks on the person, not the issue
4 · Camps & ObstructionSides form; work is blocked
5 · Threats or HarmIntimidation, retaliation or harm

Tip: Select the level supported by the behavior described. Do not assume a threat from tension alone.
